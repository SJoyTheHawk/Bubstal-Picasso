## 16-Hour Timeline

### Block 1: Hours 1-4 (Morning)
**Goal**: Core narrative + quick literature scan

#### Hour 1: Define Your Story (60 min)
- [ ] Write down in simple sentences:
  - What is the research question? (1 sentence)
  - Why does it matter academically? (2 sentences)
  - What have you already built? (2 sentences)
  - What do you need to find out? (2 sentences)
  - What are you asking from the dean? (1 sentence)

**Output**: `Core_Narrative.txt` - Your north star for the day

#### Hours 2-4: Minimum Literature Foundation (180 min)
- [ ] Search Google Scholar for 3 topics:
  - "visual complexity e-commerce conversion"
  - "image optimization online shopping"
  - "computational aesthetics marketing"

- [ ] Find 4-6 papers total, read abstracts + conclusions only
- [ ] For each paper, note:
  - What they studied
  - What they found
  - What they didn't address (your gap)

- [ ] Create ONE slide: "Current Research Landscape"
  - 3-4 bullet points of what's known
  - 2-3 bullet points of gaps
  - Your approach in one sentence

**Output**: One slide, no separate document needed

---

### Block 2: Hours 5-9 (Midday)
**Goal**: One piece of actual evidence

Pick the FASTEST option for you:

#### Option A: Platform Visual Analysis (Recommended - 4 hours)
**Do this if you can access e-commerce sites easily**

- [ ] Hour 5: Data collection
  - Pick ONE product category (e.g., "wireless earbuds")
  - Find 10 top sellers each from Amazon, Shopee, Rakuten (30 products total)
  - Save main product images

- [ ] Hours 6-7: Visual coding
  - Code 4 simple variables for each image:
    1. Background type (white/colored/lifestyle scene)
    2. Text overlay (none/minimal/heavy)
    3. Props present (yes/no)
    4. Product size in frame (small/medium/large)
  
  - Create simple spreadsheet

- [ ] Hours 8-9: Basic analysis + visualization
  - Calculate frequencies by platform
  - Create 2-3 simple bar charts
  - Note any obvious patterns

**Output**: `Platform_Analysis.pdf` (2-3 pages: method, charts, observations)

#### Option B: Picaso Output Demonstration (Alternative - 4 hours)
**Do this if Picaso is working well**

- [ ] Hour 5: Generate examples
  - Pick 2 product types
  - Generate images for all 3 platforms
  - 6 images total minimum

- [ ] Hours 6-7: Document differences
  - Screenshot comparison grids
  - Annotate what changed between platforms
  - List visual elements Picaso manipulated

- [ ] Hours 8-9: Create demonstration
  - Show input → platform-specific outputs
  - Highlight that system already quantifies visual elements
  - Explain buyer motivation mapping (B1-B7)

**Output**: `Picaso_Demo.pdf` (3-4 pages with annotated images)

#### Option C: Mini Expert Review (If desperate - 4 hours)
**Last resort if A and B aren't feasible**

- [ ] Hour 5: Prepare materials
  - Create 6-8 image pairs (platform variations)
  - Write 3 questions per pair

- [ ] Hours 6-8: Quick survey
  - Show to 10-15 people (colleagues, friends, anyone)
  - Ask: "Which would you click?" "Which platform?"
  - Record responses in simple spreadsheet

- [ ] Hour 9: Summarize
  - Calculate agreement percentages
  - Note any patterns
  - Show people CAN distinguish platform styles

**Output**: `Quick_Survey.pdf` (2 pages: method + results)

**PICK ONE OPTION AND COMMIT. DO NOT SWITCH.**

### Block 3: Hours 10-14 (Afternoon)
**Goal**: Presentation deck

#### Hours 10-12: Build Core Slides (120 min)

Create 10 slides ONLY:

1. **Title**: "Visual Complexity Quantification for E-commerce"
   - Your name, date, simple subtitle

2. **The Problem** (1 slide)
   - E-commerce visuals are designed by intuition, not data
   - Different platforms need different visual strategies
   - No systematic way to predict what works

3. **The Research Question** (1 slide)
   - "Can quantified visual elements predict customer engagement across platforms?"
   - Or simpler: "What visual patterns work on which platforms?"

4. **Why This Matters** (1 slide)
   - Academic: New methods for measuring visual complexity
   - Practical: Data-driven design for e-commerce
   - Market: Multi-billion dollar industry (cite size if you can)

5. **Current Research** (1 slide)
   - Your 4-6 papers summarized
   - The gap your work addresses

6. **Our Approach** (1 slide)
   - Quantify visual elements → Test across platforms → Build predictive model
   - Simple flow diagram

7-8. **Preliminary Evidence** (2 slides)
   - Your Block 2 work (Option A, B, or C)
   - Key finding or demonstration

9. **What We're Building** (1 slide)
   - Brief Picaso description
   - Position as research tool, not product
   - Show it proves concept feasibility

10. **Next Steps** (1 slide)
    - Phase 1: Larger-scale data collection (timeline)
    - Phase 2: Controlled experiments (timeline)
    - Phase 3: Model development (timeline)
    - What you need: [be specific - time? resources? collaboration?]

**Keep slides visual. Minimize text. One core idea per slide.**

#### Hours 12-14: Polish + Backup Materials (120 min)

- [ ] Hour 12: Create 1-page handout
  - Summarize entire presentation
  - Dean can reference during conversation
  - Include your contact info

- [ ] Hour 13: Prepare for questions
  - Write answers to:
    - "How much will this cost?"
    - "What if findings are negative?" 
    - "Who else is doing this?"
    - "How do students benefit?"
    - "What are ethical concerns?"
  - Keep answers to 2-3 sentences each

- [ ] Hour 14: Create appendix (if time)
  - Detailed Picaso technical overview
  - Full paper references
  - Extended methodology notes
  - Only if you have time; not critical

**Output**: Presentation deck + 1-page handout + Q&A prep notes

### Block 4: Hours 15-16 (End of Day)
**Goal**: Rehearse + finalize

- [ ] Refine based on practice

  - Backup on USB drive

- [ ] Print materials
  - 2 copies of handout
  - 1 copy of Q&A notes for yourself

- [ ] Pack presentation materials
  - Laptop, charger, dongles
  - Printed materials
  - Notebook for notes
  - Water bottle

- [ ] Review your core narrative one more time
  - Re-read your Hour 1 work
  - Remind yourself what you're trying to accomplish

## Meeting Day Strategy

**Before the meeting:**
- Arrive 10 minutes early
- Test technology in the room if possible
- Have water, have your notes

**Opening (2 min):**
- Thank them for time
- State the purpose: "I want to discuss a research direction around visual complexity quantification for e-commerce"
- Set expectations: "I'll present for 15-20 minutes, then I'd welcome your feedback"

**Middle (15-20 min):**
- Present your 10 slides
- Speak slowly and clearly
- Pause for questions if dean interrupts
- Don't rush through evidence slides

**Discussion (15-20 min):**
- Listen more than you talk
- Take notes on dean's concerns
- Ask: "What would need to be true for this to be worth pursuing?"
- Ask: "What concerns or gaps do you see?"

**Closing (2 min):**
- Thank them again
- Ask directly: "Do you see enough merit here to continue developing this proposal?"
- Agree on next steps (even if it's "think about it and follow up")

**After:**
- Send thank-you email within 24 hours
- Send any promised materials within 48 hours
- If dean showed interest, schedule follow-up in 1-2 weeks

## Emergency Go/No-Go Decision

**At Hour 10** (when you should have core narrative + evidence ready), assess:

### GO if:
- You have a clear research question
- You have at least one piece of evidence completed
- You understand what you're asking from the dean
- Picaso can be demonstrated if needed

### NO-GO if:
- Your research question is still unclear
- You have zero evidence (no analysis, no demo, no data)
- You don't know what you want from the meeting
- You're too exhausted to present coherently

**If NO-GO at Hour 10:** 
- Email dean immediately
- Be honest: "I need more time to prepare materials that will make good use of your time"
- Propose date 1 week out
- Use that week to do the 48-hour plan properly

**Better to postpone once than present badly.**

## Final Pep Talk

**16 hours is tight but doable.** You're not trying to prove the research is done. You're trying to prove the research is worth doing.

Focus on:
- **Clarity** over comprehensiveness
- **One good example** over many weak ones  
- **Honest assessment** over overselling
- **Productive conversation** over perfect presentation

The dean has likely seen hundreds of proposals. They're looking for:
- Is this person thinking clearly?
- Is there substance here?
- Is this worth university resources?
- Can this lead to publications/students/reputation?

You can answer those questions in 16 hours of focused work.
